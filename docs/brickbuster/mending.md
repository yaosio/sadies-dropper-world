# Mending itself

Your view is held for the whole event (Yaosio, 2026-10-10: it was too easy to miss that it fixes
itself): it follows the yarn ball out, then Sadie running after it, until she's out and the door has
shut. Then the game glides you to the far end of the room, looking at the middle of the machine so
all of it shows (`WATCH_ALL` in `room.js`), and as soon as you're there the mending starts (at most
4 s later, if something stops the glide). You're let go when it's all done. You can't walk or look
away meanwhile. On a narrow portrait phone the case's outer trim is a little cropped; the glass,
the marquee and the heap all show.

The machine fixing itself after it breaks. Read before changing it. The break is in `the-break.md`;
its timings are in `numbers.md`; its tune is in `sounds.md`.

## When

Two seconds after Sadie's out and the door's shut (about 15 seconds after the glass broke), whether
you're in the room or not (it won't be put away meanwhile). Or, if it was left broken, the first time
you're in the room (a second and a half after you come in).

## What you see and hear

To a whimsical little waltz (`sounds.md`), about 14 seconds:

1. The marquee says FIXING ITSELF. The paddle shakes off its sadness, rises out of the rubble on the
   floor, wobbles in the air and floats back into the machine (a springy boing), happy.
2. A new yarn ball pops out of the paddle (a bubbly pop), bounces up and lands on it.
3. The glass puts itself back together: every shard on the floor lifts, flies back up and the glass
   is whole again, to a run of little glass chimes. The glitter on the floor is gone.
4. The heap of knocked-out bricks dances up and out of the top of the machine, one after another:
   swaying, spinning and squashing as they go, shrinking away over the top.
5. A new board pops in, brick by brick, with the new-level jingle. The OUT OF ORDER sign comes off
   the door, the marquee shows the score again, and the case offers to play.

## What it keeps

The game carries on where it was: the same level, score, high score and lives, on a whole new board
for that level, a new ball on the paddle, no cracks and no heap. (A game from the first Brickbuster
starts at level 1 with its old score as the high score.) The save is written as the paddle goes
home, so leaving halfway through finds it mended.

## Rules

- Nothing here is loud or constant: the tune is soft and has gaps, the chimes ring and die, and the
  bricks make no sound of their own (`docs/clubhouse/RULEBOOK.md` section 4).
- It can't start while the machine is being played, and the room isn't put away until it's done.
